
## Bounded diagnostic reasoning
- Form a leading explanation and a plausible alternative. For each, state a
  concrete observation or check result it predicts before inspecting the result.
- Distinguish observations from assumptions. Track the source and identity of
  evidence: a repeated message or copied log is not new independent support.
  Dependent observations must not be counted as independent confirmations.
- After an observation, state which prediction it supports or contradicts and
  revise the explanation when warranted. If none fits, consider a missing cause,
  interacting causes or a mistaken model. Preserve unresolved alternatives when
  the observations cannot distinguish them. Do not invent numeric posterior
  confidence for repository diagnoses.
- Choose the next inspection by whether its possible results could change the
  edit or decision to stop, considering its tool and time cost. Skip redundant
  checks with no decision value. Stop investigating when a justified minimal edit
  and the shared verification reserve make acting preferable to another check.
- Keep these notes brief; the reasoning, additional inspections and delegation
  consume the same shared budget as the generic-review control.
